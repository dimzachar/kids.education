How do gears turn each other?

Turn the big gear and watch the little one.

Turn the big gear. Which way does the little gear turn?

Where the teeth meet, both gears move in the same direction, so one turns clockwise and the other anticlockwise.

Guess first!
1

Teeth push teeth

The teeth of one gear fit into the gaps of the other. When one turns, it shoves the next gear round.

2

Little gear, twice the turns

24 teeth push 12 teeth past. By the time the big gear has gone round once, the little one has gone round twice.

3

The other way round

Gears that touch always turn opposite ways. Watch the pointers: one goes clockwise, the other anticlockwise.

Build it at home: two bottle caps with corrugated cardboard glued round the rims, pinned to a box so the teeth mesh.
For grown-ups: what is really happening
  • Gear ratio. Two meshing gears turn at speeds in the inverse ratio of their tooth counts: 24 : 12 means the little gear spins 2× faster. Its torque is 2× smaller: gears trade speed for force, like levers.
  • Why the teeth don't slip. Both gears share the same tooth size (the module, 3 units here), so their pitch circles roll on each other without sliding. Real teeth have an involute shape so the push stays smooth through every angle.
  • Where you meet them. Bicycle sprockets and chains, clock movements, egg beaters, car gearboxes and wind-up toys all use the same idea to speed things up or slow them down.
