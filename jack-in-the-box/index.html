Why does Jack jump out?

Can you see the spring?

Jack is out
1

Push Jack down

Jack squashes the spring. The lid clicks shut and the latch holds it.

2

Turn the handle

The handle spins the wheel. A little peg on the wheel comes round to bump the latch.

3

The spring lets go

The peg pushes the latch open. The squashed spring stretches out and pushes Jack up. POP!

For grown-ups: what is really happening
  • Stored energy. Pushing Jack down compresses the spring. The work you do is stored as elastic potential energy (roughly ½·k·x², where k is the spring stiffness and x is how far it is squashed).
  • The latch is a catch. It holds the lid shut against the spring's push, so the energy stays trapped. Nothing moves until the latch is released.
  • The crank is a trigger, not a motor. Turning the handle spins a wheel with a peg (a simple cam). The peg only touches the latch once per turn, which is why the pop feels like a surprise.
  • Release. When the peg lifts the latch, the stored energy converts to kinetic energy almost instantly and the spring flings Jack upward. Real toys often add a music box on the same shaft, so the song plays while the peg creeps toward the latch.
