How does the well lift the bucket?

The bucket is down in the water. Pull the rope!

Pull the rope down. Where does the bucket go?

The rope runs over the wheel, so pulling one end down lifts the other end up.

Bucket in the water
1

Pull down

Pulling down is easy: you can hang your weight on the rope.

2

The wheel turns the pull around

The rope slides over the wheel, so a pull down on one side becomes a pull up on the other.

3

Up as far as you pulled

Pull the rope down a hand's length and the bucket rises a hand's length. One wheel changes direction, not distance.

Build it at home: a thread spool on a pencil, a loop of string over it, and a cup of pennies as the bucket. Pull down, cup goes up.
For grown-ups: what is really happening
  • A fixed pulley is a lever with equal arms. The wheel is a wheel-and-axle with the rope on both sides at the same radius, so the mechanical advantage is 1: you pull with the bucket's full weight, just in a more convenient direction.
  • Movable pulleys halve the force. Hang the wheel on the load instead and two rope segments share the weight: you pull half as hard, but twice as far. Block-and-tackle rigs stack these.
  • The wheel is there for friction. Without a wheel the rope would saw against the beam. A turning wheel on an axle lets the rope move with almost no loss.
