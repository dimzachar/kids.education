Why does the seesaw tip?

A big friend and a little friend. Which way will it go?

The big friend sits 2 steps out, the little friend 3 steps out. Which way does it tip?

Big friend: 2 steps × 2 weight = 4. Little friend: 3 steps × 1 = 3. Four beats three, so the big side goes down.

Heavy pushes harder

The big friend weighs twice as much, so every step he sits from the middle counts double.

2

Far out pushes harder too

Sitting further from the middle gives a longer lever. Each step out is one more push.

3

Same push, both sides: balanced

When steps × weight is the same on both ends, the plank stays level. 2 steps × 2 equals 4 steps × 1.

Build it at home: a ruler across a pencil, and coins for friends. Two coins on one side at 3 cm balance one coin at 6 cm.
For grown-ups: what is really happening
  • Torque. The turning push on a lever is force × distance from the pivot (τ = F·d). A seesaw balances when the torques on both sides are equal.
  • Why distance matters. A small weight far from the pivot can balance a large weight near it. This is the law of the lever Archimedes wrote down: F₁·d₁ = F₂·d₂.
  • Levers everywhere. Crowbars, wheelbarrows, nutcrackers, scissors and your own forearm are all levers trading force for distance the same way.
